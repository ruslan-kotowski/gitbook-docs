Miro Diagrams helps you visualize complex ideas, brainstorm effectively, and collaborate with your team in a structured way. With easy access to essential tools like **layers**, **Miro AI**, and **diagramming shapes**, this new format is your complete **workspace for diagramming**.

## What is Miro Diagrams?

Miro Diagrams is a dedicated workspace designed to enhance your diagramming experience. It provides a structured environment with tools like layers, Miro AI, and a comprehensive library of diagramming shapes. This format helps you create clear, organized, and professional diagrams for various purposes, such as process mapping, system design, or brainstorming.

## Access the Diagram format

To add a new Diagram to your Miro board, follow these steps:

1. Click the **plus (+) icon** in the toolbar.
2. Select **Diagram**.
3. Click anywhere on the canvas to add it.

This creates a dedicated format for the Diagram where you can start to:

- **Add shapes** manually.
- **Choose a template** from a selection of in-format diagram templates.
- **Create with AI**.

## Create a Diagram manually

To add shapes to your Diagram manually, follow these steps:

1. Click **Add shapes** within the Diagram format.
   This automatically opens **focus mode**, where you can:
   - Add [diagramming shapes](../../working-on-the-board/15-custom-shapes-for-diagramming.md) or [add custom shapes](../../working-on-the-board/15-custom-shapes-for-diagramming.md).
   - Connect elements with lines and arrows.
   - Add [containers](#use-containers) to group elements.
   - Add sticky notes, text, use pen tool and comments for annotating diagrams.
   - Open the [Sidekicks](../../miro-ai/07-sidekicks.md) panel.

## Start with a template

When you add a new Diagram format to the canvas, you can select a template to get started quickly. To do this:

1. Click **Start with a template** within the Diagram format.

   This automatically opens **focus mode**, where you can select one of the many templates. These templates are pre-populated with shapes and connections to help you get started faster.
2. Select the template you want to use and click **+ Add**.

   This will add the template to your Diagram, and you can start customizing it by editing shapes or adding new elements to further expand your diagram.

## Create a Diagram with AI

With [Sidekicks](../../miro-ai/07-sidekicks.md), you can generate diagrams quickly by automating shape placement and connections based on your input. Whether you're brainstorming, mapping processes, or structuring data, Miro AI saves time and ensures clarity in your visualizations.

You can use Miro AI to create diagrams in two ways depending on where you are on the canvas:

1. [From the Diagram format itself.](#create-a-diagram-from-a-diagram-format-using-ai)
2. [From the Sidekicks on the left side.](#add-a-diagram-with-miro-ai)

### **Create a diagram from a Diagram format using AI**

If you've just added a blank Diagram format to the board, you can use Miro AI to populate it:

1. Click **Create with AI** inside the Diagram. Focus mode opens.
2. Select the **diagram type** and **describe the diagram**. For example, "Online shop checkout flow".
3. Click **Generate diagram**.

:::tip
Miro AI responds to semantic styling in prompts. For more information, see [Miro AI with Diagrams and mindmaps](../../miro-ai/13-miro-ai-with-diagrams-and-mindmaps.md).
:::

Miro AI analyzes your input and generates a structured diagram based on predefined templates and connections. You can then **edit**, **move**, and **connect** AI-generated shapes just like manually added ones.

![miro.com_app_board_uXjVIP0k_DA=__focusWidget=3458764623047326338 (1).png](images/27486789892370_miro.com_app_board_uXjVIP0k_DA=__focusWidget=3458764623047326338%20(1).png)

### Create a diagram from the Create with AI panel

If you haven't added a Diagram yet, Miro AI can create one from scratch. To do this:

1. Open the **Create with AI** panel.
2. Select the **Diagram** content type.
3. Select one of the predefined **diagram types**.
4. **Describe the diagram** you want to create.
5. Select **Generate diagram**.

Your generated diagram will appear in a blank area on the canvas. You can **edit**, **move**, and **connect** AI-generated shapes just like manually added ones.

:::tip
Miro AI responds to semantic styling in prompts. For more information, see [Miro AI with Diagrams and mindmaps](../../miro-ai/13-miro-ai-with-diagrams-and-mindmaps.md).
:::

## Create a Diagram format from an existing diagram

If you've already created a diagram on the canvas and want to structure it into a Diagram format, you can convert it with one click. This keeps all connections intact while adding Diagram-specific functionality.

To convert an existing diagram on the canvas into a Diagram format:

1. Select **multiple connected shapes**.
2. Click **Wrap as diagram** in the context menu.

Your selected shapes and connections will be structured into a Diagram. You can still move shapes in and out of the Diagram format.

![Converting existing connected shapes into a Diagram format using the Wrap as diagram option.](images/25389869362322_wrap-as-diagram.png)

## Work on your Diagram

Once you've added or generated a Diagram, you can refine it using **Focus mode, Layers, and the Context menu**. These tools help you stay organized, minimize distractions, and efficiently structure your diagram.

### Use Focus mode

Diagrams can be opened in **focus mode** (full screen) when you want to focus solely on the Diagram you're working on and not other content on the board. Focus mode is the diagramming workspace in Miro designed for efficient diagramming. To access this mode, click on the **Focus mode** icon above the Diagram you're working on.

![Focus mode icon above a Diagram.](images/25407630835090_miro.com_app_board_uXjVIP0k_DA=_%20(3) copy.png)

While in focus mode, you can access all Diagram editing tools, including shape customization, layers, AI-powered generation, and the pen tool for freehand drawing and annotations. Additionally, **shapes snap to a grid**, making it easier to align elements precisely. You can also use Precise resizing in Focus mode, allowing you to specify pixel dimensions for widgets within your Diagram. For more information, see [Precise resizing](../../essential-tools/01-precise-resizing.md).

The shapes panel remembers its open or closed state between sessions, so your workspace stays configured the way you left it.

To exit focus mode, click **Back to Canvas** at the top of the screen.

### Use layers

Layers in Diagrams allow you to **organize and manage content** within a Diagram without affecting the rest of the board. Unlike [canvas layers](../../essential-tools/09-layers.md), which apply to the entire Miro board, **Diagram layers are specific to the Diagram you're working on**.

Layers in Diagrams work as follows:

- Layers can only be managed within the Diagram's **focus mode**. You can quickly access focus mode by clicking on any shape within the diagram and then selecting the "You are editing: Diagram layer name" button.
- Each Diagram has its **own set of layers**, separate from the canvas layers.
- Each Diagram starts with a **default layer**, where all new elements are placed. If needed, you can create additional layers to better structure complex Diagrams.
- You can **add multiple layers** to structure your Diagram.
- **Elements** inside a Diagram **can be moved between layers**.
- You can **hide or lock layers** to focus on specific parts of the Diagram while editing or presenting.
- If you copy and paste or duplicate a Diagram, all of its layers will be duplicated as well. This is particularly useful for creating different versions of a diagram without having to recreate the layer structure each time.

#### Add a new layer

To create a new layer inside a Diagram, follow these steps:

1. Open the Diagram's **focus mode**.
2. Click **Layers** in the top-right corner to open the **Diagram layers** sidebar.
3. Click **+ New layer** to create a new layer.
4. Rename the layer (optional) to keep your Diagram organized.

Alternatively, you can right-click an element on a diagram and then select **Move to layer** > **+ New layer**.

#### Manage layers

By default, all new elements are placed in the **default layer**. To better organize your Diagram, you can move elements between layers, hide or lock layers, and adjust layer settings.

**Move elements between layers:**

To move elements between layers:

1. Right-click the element(s) you want to move.
2. Select **Move to layer** and then select the layer you want to move your element to.

**Hide layers:**

Hiding layers can help simplify your view while editing or presenting a Diagram. To hide a layer:

- In the **Diagram layers sidebar**, click the **eye icon** next to a layer to hide or show it.

**Lock layers:**

Locking a layer prevents accidental edits. To lock a layer:

- In the **Diagram layers sidebar**, click the **lock icon** next to a layer to disable modifications. Click it again to unlock.

**Modify layer settings:**

You can rename, duplicate, move, or delete layers. To modify layer settings:

- In the **Diagram layers sidebar**, click the **three-dots icon** next to a layer to access additional options.

### Use containers

Containers are similar to frames but are optimized for diagramming. They help you group and organize elements on your canvas. With containers, connector lines work seamlessly, and you can nest containers within each other.

There are two main types of containers you can use:

- **Generic containers**: These function like frames and act as parents to all the elements placed inside them. You can name these containers to define specific regions within your diagram. You can also nest generic containers to create subsections, which is useful for managing more complex diagrams.
- **Swimlanes**: These are primarily used for process diagrams to visually separate different lanes of work, such as different departments or stages in a process.

For additional organization, you can move containers to different [layers](#context-menu-in-diagrams) within your diagram.

### Diagram context menu

When working on Diagrams, you can find additional functions through the context menu, accessed by clicking the three dots (...) icon above the Diagram on the canvas or in the top-right corner of focus mode.

The context menu provides the following options:

- **Copy focus mode link**: Copy a direct link to the Diagram in focus mode to share with collaborators.
- **Rename**: Change the name of your Diagram to keep it organized, especially when a board contains multiple Diagrams.
- **Assign (or remove) status**: Diagrams can have a status label (for example, **Draft**) displayed next to the Diagram name. Use this to communicate the current state of a Diagram to your team. Select **Remove status** to clear it.
- **Set as default view**: Set the Diagram as the default view when opening the board. The selected Diagram will automatically open in focus mode every time the board is opened.
- **Export as PDF**: Export the Diagram directly as a PDF file.
- **Export as image**: Export the Diagram as JPG, SVG, or PDF. See Export your [Diagram as image](#export-your-diagram-as-image) for details.
- **Info**: View Diagram metadata such as **Created by** and **Last modified by**.

## Add your Diagram to a Doc

You can add a [synced copy](../../working-on-the-board/11-synced-copies.md) of your Diagram to a [Doc in Miro](../12-docs-in-miro.md) to provide context, structure, and visual clarity to your documents. This is especially useful when you need to share your Diagram with others or present it in a more structured format.

To add a Diagram to your Doc, follow these steps:

1. **Select** the Diagram on the canvas.
2. **Drag and drop** your Diagram into a Doc.
3. **Resize and position** your Diagram within the Doc as needed.

Your synced copy of the Diagram is now part of the Doc.

![Dragging and dropping a Diagram into a Miro Doc to create a synced copy.](images/25569793628306_miro.com_app_board_uXjVIP0k_DA=_%20(6).png)
:::note
Synced copies are **view-only** for everyone. To change the copy content, modify the content of the original Diagram.
:::

## Copy your Diagram to another board

When multiple teams collaborate across different boards, you can share the same Diagram in multiple places by creating a [synced copy](../../working-on-the-board/11-synced-copies.md). This ensures everyone stays aligned around a single source of truth, no matter where they're working.

To copy a synced Diagram to another board, follow these steps:

1. **Right-click** the Diagram you want to share.
2. Select **Copy and sync**.
3. Navigate to the target board and **paste** the Diagram.

The copied Diagram will stay **synchronized with the original**—any changes made to the source will be reflected automatically in the synced copy.

:::note
Synced copies are **view-only** for everyone. To change the copy content, modify the content of the original Diagram.
:::

## Share your Diagram

There are a few ways to share your Diagram with others:

1. **Copy the URL** from the address bar of your browser while in focus mode.
2. **Click the three-dots (...) menu** and select **Copy focus mode link**.

## Export your Diagram as image

You can export your Diagram as JPG, PDF, or SVG.

To export your diagram, follow these steps:

1. Select your diagram on the canvas, or open it in focus mode.
2. Click the vertical three-dots to open the **More** menu.
3. Click **Export as image** (for JPG, SVG, or PDF) or **Export as PDF**.
   For JPG, you can optionally select the resolution as **Small**, (Default) **Medium**, or **Large**.
4. Click **Export**.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I import diagrams from other tools like Lucidchart, Draw.io, or Visio?**

Yes! You can easily import diagrams from other platforms and convert them into Miro's Diagram format.

We support the following workflows:

- **Lucidchart**: You can either export your Lucidchart diagram as a **.vsdx** file and import it into Miro, or simply copy and paste directly from Lucidchart into your Miro board.
- **Draw.io** (also known as diagrams.net): Export your diagram as a **.vsdx** file and import it into Miro.
- **Microsoft Visio**: Export your diagram from Visio as a **.vsdx** file, then import it into Miro.
- **OmniGraffle**: Export your diagram as a **.graffle** file and import it into Miro.

Once imported, **select the shapes**, then use [**Wrap as diagram**](#create-a-diagram-format-from-an-existing-diagram) from the context menu to convert them into a structured Diagram format.
